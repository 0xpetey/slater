# Two OCR readings, not Vision's confidence score, decide Low-confidence Blocks

Measured on the test fixtures, Vision's confidence score doesn't reflect accuracy. It gave 1.00 to a Line with a dropped character (取り消す read as 取りす) and 0.30–0.50 to correctly read short words like 品番. Preprocessing is also a double-edged tool: upscaling and contrast rescue small text and blurry scans, but make Vision drop characters from clean text (担当者 read as 担者). So Slater reads every crop twice. Where the readings agree the text is trusted. Where they differ, the longer reading wins, because dropped characters are the usual failure, and the Block is marked Low-confidence. This roughly doubles OCR work, and that's the price of catching misreads that would otherwise be silent.

**Updated 2026-09-23, for speed.** The two readings are no longer symmetric. Vision's language correction is the most expensive step in the whole pipeline (a 20-line crop takes about 1.2 s with it and 0.5 s without), so the *quick* reading uses the raw image with correction off and is what the Shot shows first, while the *corrected* reading uses correction and verifies the Shot once it lands, a little later. Ties now go to the corrected reading, whose misreads are rarer (without correction, 末 came back as 未 on blurry scans). The user may briefly see a translation from the quick reading that the corrected one then replaces; that Block is marked Low-confidence, as before.

**Updated 2026-09-23, for 当.** In gothic Japanese fonts (Hiragino Sans, macOS's default, and Tsukushi Gothic) at Retina scale, Vision glues 当 to the character beside it and reads the pair as one glyph (該当 → 該, 当社 → 社), or reads a lone 当 as 肖. Nothing in the request changes this (language list, correction, compute device, the document recognizer), but Vision reads the same text correctly at half scale, where the per-character boxes prove the mechanism: a merged glyph's box is about twice as wide as its neighbors'. So on Retina captures the corrected reading is now the crop at **half scale** rather than upscaled, and every Line carries a **suspected-merge flag** (one full-width character's box more than 1.6× the line's median: 101 correct flags and 1 false one over 288 lines). Reconciliation prefers the reading without a suspected merge, then the longer text, then the corrected reading. Measured over six fonts and three sizes, that took 241 of 288 lines right to 258, without losing ground on ordinary pages or 9pt text; the half-scale reading also crispens blur. The upscaled, contrast-enhanced reading remains for 1× captures, where text is too small to shrink.
