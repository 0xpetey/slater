# Two OCR readings, not Vision's confidence score, decide Low-confidence Blocks

Measured on the test fixtures, Vision's confidence score doesn't reflect accuracy. It gave 1.00 to a Line with a dropped character (取り消す read as 取りす) and 0.30–0.50 to correctly read short words like 品番. Preprocessing is also a double-edged tool: upscaling and contrast rescue small text and blurry scans, but make Vision drop characters from clean text (担当者 read as 担者). So Slater reads every crop twice. Where the readings agree the text is trusted. Where they differ, the longer reading wins, because dropped characters are the usual failure, and the Block is marked Low-confidence. This roughly doubles OCR work, and that's the price of catching misreads that would otherwise be silent.

**Updated 2026-09-23, for speed.** The two readings are no longer symmetric. Vision's language correction is the most expensive step in the whole pipeline (a 20-line crop takes about 1.2 s with it and 0.5 s without), so the *quick* reading uses the raw image with correction off and is what the Shot shows first, while the *corrected* reading uses the preprocessed image with correction on and verifies the Shot once it lands, a little later. Ties now go to the corrected reading, whose misreads are rarer (without correction, 末 came back as 未 on blurry scans). The user may briefly see a translation from the quick reading that the corrected one then replaces; that Block is marked Low-confidence, as before.
